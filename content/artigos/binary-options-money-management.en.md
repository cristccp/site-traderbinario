**The best money management for binary options is the one you set before you enter and follow when things go wrong.** In practice, it has four rules: how much to risk per trade, the daily stop, how many trades to take and when to withdraw.

Strategy decides how you enter. **Risk management decides whether you stay.** And in binary options it matters even more, because every loss takes the whole amount of the trade.

In this article I show you how to set up these four rules with simple numbers, and why martingale, doubling the amount to win back a loss, is the fastest shortcut to an empty account.

## Why binary options *need* their own money management

A lot of money management content was written for forex or stocks, with a stop-loss on the chart and leverage. In binary options, neither works the same way:

- **There is no stop-loss inside the trade.** The amount you put in is already the maximum loss. Lose, and you lose everything in that trade.
- **A win pays less than a loss costs.** That is why winning half of your trades is not enough to break even. I show this math in [what binary options are](/en/what-are-binary-options).
- **Everything is fast.** Results in minutes pull you toward impulse. That is where the balance slips away.

So money management in binary options is not about where to place the stop. It is about **how much goes into each trade and when you stop**.

## Rule 1: a *small, fixed* amount per trade

Because the trade amount is the risk itself, it has to be small compared with the balance. A conservative starting point is **around 1% of the balance per trade**.

**Example:** a $500 balance → $5 per trade.

And the amount is **fixed**: it does not go up because you feel confident, and it does not go up to win back a loss. See what a streak of 8 losses in a row, which happens to every trader, does to the balance:

| Risk per trade | After 8 losses in a row |
|---|---|
| 1% | about −8%: you can keep going and learn |
| 2% | about −16%: it shakes you emotionally |
| 5% | about −40%: desperation mode |

Risk management is, above all, emotional management. At 1%, a bad streak is a bad day. At 5%, it becomes the start of the cycle that blows accounts.

## Rule 2: the *daily stop*

The daily stop is the loss limit that ends your day. You decide it before opening the platform, never in the middle of a losing streak.

**Example:** at $5 per trade, stop after **2 or 3 losses** ($10 to $15). Hit it, close the platform.

It is worth repeating: **the most dangerous time of the day is right after you hit the stop.** That is when your head wants to win it back, and the trader enters badly, enters bigger and loses more.

Two locks that help:

- **A break after 2 losses in a row:** stand up, breathe, 10 to 15 minutes away from the screen, or end the day.
- **The daily target as a stopping point:** the target is not a profit forecast. It is the time to stop when the day went well, before you give it back.

## Rule 3: *few* trades

Overtrading does not blow the account all at once. It bleeds it slowly.

- **Beginners or impulsive traders:** few trades a day and few days a week.
- **With experience and discipline:** you can be more flexible, as long as the limit is still set beforehand.
- **Pick your time:** trading in a fixed time window keeps you from staying all day looking for entries.

If you need to trade to feel good, you have already lost control.

## Rule 4: *withdrawals* with a routine

Profit you do not withdraw is an illusion. It stays on the platform until the next bad streak takes it back.

The method's guideline is to have a routine: **at least 1 to 2 withdrawals a week** from the day trading balance for those who are starting out or usually give back what they made. With experience, you can adjust it. What you cannot do is have no rule.

## Martingale: why doubling up *blows the account*

Martingale means doubling the amount after each loss to win everything back at once. It sounds logical. The math shows the opposite:

| Trade | Amount ($500 balance) | Total already risked |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | $5 | $5 |
| 2nd | $10 | $15 |
| 3rd | $20 | $35 |
| 4th | $40 | $75 |
| 5th | $80 | $155 |
| 6th | $160 | $315 |

After 6 losses in a row, you have put **63% of the balance** on the line to try to win back $5. And because a win pays less than a loss costs, doubling does not always cover the accumulated loss.

Streaks of 5 or 6 losses are not rare. For beginners, martingale turns a bad day into an empty account.

## How to build *your* money management

:::na-pratica
1. **Set the balance** with an amount you can afford to lose. A day trading balance is not your savings.
2. **Amount per trade:** around 1% of the balance, fixed.
3. **Daily stop:** 2 or 3 losses and you are done. A break after 2 in a row.
4. **Limit of trades** per day and days per week, before you start.
5. **Weekly withdrawal routine**, even a small one.
6. **Log everything:** time, asset, amount, result and whether you followed the plan.
7. **Practice on the demo account** until you follow the rules for a few weeks in a row.
:::

## *Summary*

- In binary options, the trade amount is the whole risk: it has to be small and fixed, around 1% of the balance.
- Daily stop set beforehand, with a break after 2 losses in a row. After the stop, the platform stays closed.
- Few trades, in a fixed time window.
- A weekly withdrawal routine.
- Martingale multiplies risk and is the fastest way to empty the account.

The market does not reward whoever wins the most. It rewards whoever lasts the longest. Trade with method.
