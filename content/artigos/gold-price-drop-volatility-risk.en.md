**Gold had its biggest weekly drop in eight months: about 9% in the week to November 20, 2026.** The following Monday, it rose more than 2%. That is not a forecast of anything. It is a snapshot of a market that sped up.

And a fast market does not call for courage. **It calls for a smaller position.** If you trade the usual size while gold moves three times more per day, you are taking three times the risk without noticing.

In this article I show you what moved the price, what that week teaches about "safe assets" and the simple math to adjust position size and stop when gold volatility rises.

## What *happened* to gold

The facts, with dates, according to financial news reports:

- **November 20, 2026 (Friday):** spot gold fell 2.7% on the day, to about $3,525 an ounce, and closed the week down about 9%. It was the lowest level in more than a year. In January, the record had gone above $5,500 ([The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/money/2026/11/20/why-is-gold-price-going-down/)).
- **November 23, 2026 (Monday):** gold rose about 2.4%, to near $3,620 an ounce ([Diário Económico](https://www.diarioeconomico.co.mz/2026/11/23/mercados/commodities/ouro-avanca-241-apos-a-maior-queda-semanal-em-oito-meses/)).

Prices change all the time. If you are reading this later, check the current quote on your platform.

## Why the price *dropped*

The reports point to a mix of factors:

- **US interest rates:** expectations that the Fed would raise rates again weighed on gold. Higher rates make an asset that pays no interest, like gold, less attractive.
- **A stronger dollar:** gold is priced in dollars. A strong dollar usually weighs on it.
- **Forced selling:** as stock markets fell, especially AI-related shares, many investors sold gold to raise cash and cover margin. Gold became a source of cash, not a shelter.

On Monday, part of the move came back as expectations of a December rate hike eased and buyers stepped in at lower prices.

Notice: **none of these reasons were on the 5-minute chart.** If you were only watching candles, you took the hit without knowing where it came from.

## "Safe havens" *fall* too

Gold has a reputation as a safe harbor. For long-term holders, that reputation has history behind it. For short-term traders using leverage, it can be misleading.

A 9% drop in a week, in the price of gold, is a lot of money per contract. And with leverage, what is a scare on the chart can be the end of a small account.

## The math: more *volatility*, smaller size

On XAU/USD, 1 standard lot is usually 100 ounces. So with **0.01 lot**, every $1 move in the gold price is worth about **$1** in your account. Check the contract size with your broker, because it can vary.

Now look at what that week did:

| Move | In price | With 0.01 lot | With 0.10 lot |
|---|---|---|---|
| One day down 2.7% | about $98 | about $98 | about $980 |
| The week down ~9% | about $357 | about $357 | about $3,570 |

With a $1,000 account and 0.10 lot, a single day like that takes almost the whole account. **The problem was not gold. It was the size.**

The rule that fixes it never changes:

:::na-pratica
1. **Set your risk in money first:** around 1% of the account per trade. $1,000 account → $10.
2. **Place the stop where it makes sense** in a choppy market. If gold moves $100 in a day, a $5 stop is noise and will be hit for no reason.
3. **Work out the size:** risk in money ÷ stop distance. A wider stop with the same risk = a smaller position.
4. **If the minimum size (0.01) already risks more than your limit, do not trade.** With $10 of risk, 0.01 lot only fits a $10 stop in price. On a $100 day, that is not a trade, it is a coin flip.
:::

If you trade short-term options instead of CFDs, the same idea applies: the amount you put in each trade is your maximum loss, so in a jumpy market it should stay small and fixed. See [the best money management for binary options](/en/blog/binary-options-money-management).

## What *not* to do after a sharp drop

**1. Trying to catch the bottom.** "It fell 9%, it can only go up now" is a hunch. Nobody knows where the price stops. On Monday it rose. It could have fallen further.

**2. Sizing up to win it back.** If the drop caught you, the urge is to go bigger and recover everything at once. That is how a bad day becomes an empty account.

**3. Trading without a stop "because gold always comes back".** It has come back from drops before. That says nothing about how deep the hole gets along the way, or about the size of your account.

## *Summary*

- Gold fell about 9% in the week to November 20, 2026, its biggest weekly drop in eight months, and rose about 2.4% the following Monday.
- US rates, a strong dollar and forced selling to cover margin pushed the price down.
- "Safe havens" fall too, and with leverage the drop weighs much more.
- A choppier market calls for a wider stop and, for the same risk, a smaller position.
- If even the minimum size does not fit your risk, stay out or practice on a demo account.

The market will still be there tomorrow. Your account will only be there if the size is right. Protect your capital.
