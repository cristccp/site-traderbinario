**Setting up a trading bot means deciding, before you hit play, how much it can risk and when it stops.** There are five safeguards: a separate balance, trade size, stop, target and how many bots run at the same time.

A lot of people think the bot decides whether the account survives. It does not. **The bot executes. The settings decide the risk.** A bot without rules blows an account just like a trader without rules, only faster.

In this article I show you the five settings that come before hitting play, an example with simple numbers, the mistakes that wreck accounts with bots and how to test everything on a demo account.

## Why the *settings* matter more than the bot

Once the bot is ready, what separates an account that lasts from one that hits zero is how you configured it.

- **The bot does not know how much you can afford to lose.** It trades the amount you set, as many times as you allow.
- **The bot does not know when to stop.** Without a stop and a target, it keeps going until the balance runs out.
- **The bot has no impulse. You do.** The biggest risk is you changing the settings mid-day to "win it back".

A good bot is not one that is always right. It is one that knows when to stop. And you are the one who teaches it that.

## The 5 settings *before hitting play*

:::na-pratica
1. **Separate balance.** Set aside an amount just for the bot, money you can lose without touching your monthly bills. A trading account is not a savings account.
2. **Small, fixed trade size.** A conservative starting point is around 1% of the balance per trade. Fixed: it does not go up after a win and it does not go up to recover a loss.
3. **Stop.** How much the bot can lose before it stops on its own that day. Hit it, switch off.
4. **Target.** The stopping point on a good day. A target is not a profit forecast: it is when you stop before giving it back.
5. **How many bots at once.** Every bot you turn on adds risk. Three bots with the same trade size risk three times as much.
:::

Notice that none of these settings is about strategy. They are about **how much** and **when to stop**. It is the same logic as [money management for binary options](/en/blog/binary-options-money-management), just written into the platform before you start.

## An example with *simple numbers*

Picture a $500 balance set aside just for bots.

| Setting | Example | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Bot balance | $500 | an amount you can afford to lose |
| Trade size | $5 | around 1% of the balance |
| Daily stop | $15 | 3 losses in a row and the bot stops |
| Daily target | $10 | a good day ends before you give it back |
| Bots at once | 2, with $2.50 each | risk is split, not multiplied |

With these settings, a bad day takes $15, about 3% of the balance. You can keep going, review and adjust calmly.

Now the opposite: a $25 trade size (5% of the balance), three bots running and no stop. A streak of 4 losses on each bot takes $300, or 60% of the balance, before you even notice. Same bot, same market. **Different settings, different outcome.**

## The 4 mistakes that *wreck accounts* with bots

**1. Going straight to a live account.** Every new setup runs on demo first. That is where you find out the stop was too wide or that two bots trade at the same moment.

**2. Tweaking in the middle of the day.** The bot lost twice, so you double the trade size "just for today". That is martingale dressed up as an adjustment. It is the same impulse a manual trader has, just automated.

**3. Adding bots without splitting the trade size.** Each extra bot feels like diversification. Without splitting the amount, it is just more risk switched on at once.

**4. Thinking the bot needs no monitoring.** The bot does not get tired, but the market changes. Check your log, look at the losing days and adjust outside trading hours, never in the heat of the moment.

## Test it on *demo* before you trust it

Before putting money in, run the setup on a demo account for a few weeks. A backtest shows how the bot behaved in the past. The demo shows how **you** behave with it running.

Write down three things each week: how many times the stop was hit, how many times the target was hit and whether you changed the settings outside the plan. If the last answer is "yes", the problem is not the bot.

And do not forget withdrawals. A bot does not change the rule: profit you never withdraw is an illusion. For beginners, the method's guideline is a routine of at least 1 to 2 withdrawals a week from the trading balance.

## *Summary*

- The bot executes. The settings you choose before turning it on decide the risk.
- Five safeguards before hitting play: separate balance, small fixed trade size, stop, target and how many bots.
- Several bots add up risk: split the trade size between them.
- Do not change the settings mid-day. Adjust outside trading hours, based on your log.
- Every new setup runs on a demo account first.

A bot without rules blows the account. Rules before play. Trade with method.
