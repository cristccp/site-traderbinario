**Reading candlesticks means looking at each candle and understanding three things: who won, how strongly and where price was rejected.** The body, the wicks and the color already tell you that. You do not need to memorize forty names.

Most people do the opposite. They memorize patterns, spot a "hammer" anywhere on the chart and jump in. **A candle is not an entry signal. It is a reading of the fight between buyers and sellers in that minute.** And a reading without context turns into an excuse to trade.

In this article I show you how a candle is built, the 3 questions to read any of them, the 5 patterns worth knowing and where they really carry weight.

## How a *candle* is built

Each candle sums up a period of time. On a 1-minute chart, each candle is 1 minute. On a 5-minute chart, each candle is 5 minutes. Inside it there are four prices:

- **Open:** the price at the start of the period.
- **Close:** the price at the end of the period.
- **High:** the highest point price touched.
- **Low:** the lowest point price touched.

The **body** runs from the open to the close. The **wicks** (or shadows) are the thin lines above and below the body: they show how far price went and could not stay.

The **color** tells you the direction. On most platforms, green (or white) closed above the open. Red (or black) closed below it.

## The 3 *questions* to read any candle

:::na-pratica
1. **Who won?** Look at the color. Green: buyers finished ahead. Red: sellers finished ahead.
2. **How strongly?** Compare the body size with the previous candles. A big body means a decision. A small body means doubt.
3. **Where was price rejected?** Look at the wicks. A long lower wick: price dropped and got pushed back up. A long upper wick: price rose and got pushed back down.
:::

With these 3 questions you can read any candle, even one without a name. A pattern's name is just a shortcut to that reading.

## The 5 *patterns* that matter

| Pattern | What it looks like | What it usually shows |
|---|---|---|
| Hammer | Small body on top, long lower wick | Sellers pushed, buyers pushed back: low prices rejected |
| Shooting star | Small body at the bottom, long upper wick | Buyers pushed, sellers pushed back: high prices rejected |
| Engulfing | A big body that covers the whole previous body, in the opposite color | A change of control: the side that was losing took over |
| Doji | Open and close almost the same, body almost gone | A tie: nobody won, the market is undecided |
| Strong-body candle | Big body, short wicks | One side dominated the whole period |

Notice that all of them answer the same 3 questions. The hammer, for example, is just a candle with a strong rejection at the bottom. The doji is a candle with no winner. Once you get that, you do not need a cheat sheet.

## Where a pattern *counts* (and where it does not)

This is what separates reading from guessing. **The same candle means different things depending on where it shows up.**

- **At a support or resistance zone:** a hammer at a support that has held price before carries weight. It is the market reacting again where it already reacted. If you do not draw those zones yet, start with [support and resistance](/en/blog/support-and-resistance-how-to-draw).
- **In the middle of nowhere:** the same hammer far from any zone is just a candle. No reference. Time to wait.
- **After a stretched move:** an engulfing candle after several candles in the same direction can show fatigue. Sometimes it is only a pause and the move continues.
- **A doji is a warning, not an entry.** It tells you nobody is in charge. Entering a tie is just picking the side you are rooting for.

A practical tip: look at a higher timeframe first, such as 15 minutes, to see the context, and only then at the candle on the timeframe you trade. If you use the [TradingView chart on Ideal Trade](/en/blog/tradingview-chart-light-vs-advanced), you can switch timeframes with one click.

## The 3 *mistakes* that cost the most

**1. Memorizing patterns instead of reading the candle.** If you memorize, you see patterns everywhere. And if you see patterns everywhere, you trade all day long.

**2. Entering while the candle is still open.** Until the candle closes, it can still turn into a different shape. The nice hammer at 40 seconds can close as a strong bearish candle. Wait for the close.

**3. Raising your trade size because "the pattern is perfect".** A nice-looking pattern does not change your trade size. In a binary option, the amount you put in is already the maximum loss on that trade. The candle helps you decide where. **Your money management decides how much.**

If you have not set that number yet, read [the best money management for binary options](/en/blog/binary-options-money-management) before your next trade.

## Reading candles makes you *trade less*

When you stop hunting for pattern names and start asking the 3 questions, something happens: most candles tell you nothing. And that is fine. A candle with no rejection, no strength and far from any zone is a signal to wait.

For beginners, this is worth a lot: few trades, only when the candle and the zone tell the same story, with your amount and daily stop set beforehand. Trading less is what keeps you in the market.

## *Summary*

- Every candle shows open, close, high and low. The body and the wicks show the fight between buyers and sellers.
- Read any candle with 3 questions: who won, how strongly and where price was rejected.
- Five patterns are enough to start: hammer, shooting star, engulfing, doji and the strong-body candle.
- A pattern only carries weight at a support or resistance zone, and with the candle closed.
- A nice-looking pattern does not change your trade size: money management decides that.

Fewer pattern names, more reading. Trade with method.
