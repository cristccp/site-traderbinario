**A binary option is a trade where you choose whether an asset's price will be above or below a point at the end of a short time frame.** If you are right, you receive a payout set before you enter. If you are wrong, you lose the whole amount of the trade. There are only two outcomes, and that is where the name "binary" comes from.

It sounds simple. And that simplicity is exactly what blows up so many accounts. In this guide I will show you how a trade really works, the math almost no one shows, the real risks and how to start the right way, with a demo account.

![Candlestick chart of a financial asset on a trading platform screen](/imagens/o-que-sao-opcoes-binarias/01.webp "1200x800")

## How a *trade* works, step by step

Every binary option trade has the same five elements:

1. **Asset:** what you are going to analyze. It can be a currency pair (like EUR/USD), gold, an index or, at some brokers, an OTC or synthetic asset.
2. **Direction:** you choose **up** (the price will be above the entry point) or **down** (it will be below). On many platforms, the buttons show up as "Higher/Lower", "Rise/Fall" or "Call/Put".
3. **Expiry:** the length of the trade, from a few seconds to a few hours.
4. **Amount:** how much you put into the trade. This is exactly the amount you lose if you are wrong.
5. **Payout:** what the broker pays if you are right, shown as a percentage before you enter. It changes with the asset and the time of day.

**Example:** you put $10 on an up trade on EUR/USD, with a 5-minute expiry and an 85% payout.

- If, after 5 minutes, the price is above the entry point, you get your $10 back **plus $8.50**.
- If it is below, you **lose the $10**.

Notice the detail: a win pays $8.50, but a loss costs $10.

## The math *almost no one* shows

Because a win pays less than a loss costs, winning half of your trades is not enough. With an 85% payout, **you need to win more than 54% of your trades just to avoid losing money**.

| Payout | Win rate needed just to break even |
|---|---|
| 90% | about 53% |
| 85% | about 54% |
| 80% | about 56% |

And that is without counting the most expensive mistake of all: raising the amount to win back a loss. That is how a bad day turns into an empty account.

That is why the right question is not "which strategy wins more often?". It is **"how much do I risk per trade and when do I stop?"**. That has a name: risk management. I explain how to set yours up in [the best money management for binary options](/en/blog/binary-options-money-management).

## The most *common* types

The names change from broker to broker, but the formats are similar:

- **Up or down:** the most common. Will the price be above or below the entry point at expiry?
- **Touch / no touch:** will the price touch, or not, a set level during the trade?
- **In or out:** will the price end inside or outside a price range?
- **Very short time frames:** trades of 60 seconds or less. They are the fastest and the ones that pull hardest toward impulse.

Some platforms also offer **digital options**, which work in a similar way but with their own payout rules. I explain the difference in [binary vs digital options](/en/blog/binary-vs-digital-options).

## Do binary options *work*? Is it gambling?

These are two of the most searched questions on the topic, so I will be direct.

**The mechanism works** exactly as the broker shows it. What does not exist is a guaranteed way to win. Nobody is right every time, and the payout math works against anyone who trades a lot and without criteria.

**The structure looks like a bet:** fixed amount, short time frame, all or nothing. That is why, without a method and without management, it becomes a bet in practice. What separates one from the other is not the asset or the platform. It is behavior: how much you risk, how many trades you take, whether you follow a plan and whether you know when to stop.

## The *real* risks

- **Total loss per trade.** Lose, and you lose everything you put into that trade. There is no "losing just a little".
- **Speed.** Results in minutes are addictive. The urge for "just one more" is the start of the cycle that blows accounts.
- **OTC assets.** At night and on weekends, many brokers offer assets whose price is formed by the broker itself, not by the global market.
- **Regulation.** Many brokers that offer binary options operate offshore, without authorization from the regulator in your country, and regulators in several countries have issued warnings against them. In practice, you have less protection if something goes wrong.
- **Scams.** "Guaranteed" signal groups, miracle robots, an "account manager" who trades your account, promises of so many percent a day. If someone promised profit, be suspicious.

## Binary options *or* forex?

They are different markets. In a binary option, the outcome is fixed and the time frame is set when you enter. In forex, the outcome varies with the price move, you choose when to exit and you use a stop to limit the loss. Both involve high risk.

## How to start *the right way*

A binary option is not an investment and it is not an income. If you want to learn to trade, start like this:

:::na-pratica
1. **Demo account first.** Practice with practice money until you understand the mechanism and your own reaction to losses.
2. **Risk management set before the first trade:** how much you risk per trade (a small part of the balance) and your daily stop. Hit it, stop.
3. **Few trades.** For beginners, trading fewer times a week protects you more than staying in front of the screen all day.
4. **Pick your time.** The chart changes throughout the day. Trading in a fixed time window keeps you from hunting for entries all day.
5. **Log everything and withdraw.** Write down every trade and, when there is profit, withdraw part of it. Profit you do not withdraw is an illusion.
:::

## *Summary*

- A binary option is an all-or-nothing trade, with the time frame and payout set when you enter.
- A win pays less than a loss costs. With an 85% payout, you need to win more than 54% just to break even.
- Without a method and management, it becomes a bet in practice.
- Many brokers operate offshore, without authorization in your country: less protection for you.
- Start with a demo account, with management set beforehand and few trades.

Consistency is behavior, not strategy. Trade with method.
